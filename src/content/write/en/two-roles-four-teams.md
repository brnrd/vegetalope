For the past few weeks, I’ve been covering the engineering manager role for one of my four teams. I’ll be doing that alongside my usual job as Head of Engineering for a few months.

I’m back to working day to day with a Product Manager and a Product Designer, having 1:1s with the engineers and coordinating with other teams. I’ve been Head of Engineering for a little over four years, which doesn’t feel all that long. I’d almost forgotten how much of the week cross-team coordination can take.

I have a few advantages this time around. I know most of the people I need to speak to, I have access to more information and, while my own manager is on leave, I’m working directly with our CTO. Holding both roles also means I can propose a change to the team and make a call that one of my engineering managers would usually talk through with me first.

The team had been following the same Scrum routine for a long time. They were open to trying something that suited the pace they work at and reflected how much of the work they already organise themselves. Today, four weeks after I stepped in, we switched to Scrumban. That takes more than three hours of meetings a week off our calendars. I’m curious to see how it feels after a couple of months.

I’m used to dividing my attention across teams. Doing it well is harder when I’m managing one of them day to day. I can get caught up in what’s happening with that team and realise I’ve given the other three very little time. A day spent helping the other teams can leave me catching up with the one I’m managing directly.

I’ve always been realistic about what I can fit into a working week. Doing both jobs means making harder choices about where my time will help most. Some worthwhile work has to wait longer than I’d like. Sometimes, when we pick it up again, we find there’s a simpler way forward.

It helps that the engineers are experienced and most have worked here for a long time. They know when to get on with something and when to bring someone else in. I’ve worked with the other engineering managers for between one and three years, and I trust them to make decisions for their teams and tell me early if something needs my attention.

I’m glad I get to spend a few months back in the job. Working directly with a team gives me a fresh perspective on how I manage, from listening in a 1:1 to helping someone work through a problem. There’s always more to learn from those conversations.
